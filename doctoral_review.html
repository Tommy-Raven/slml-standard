SLML is a design-time structural admissibility standard. It determines, through a machine-evaluable manifest, whether a system is structurally permitted to claim it serves its users. Its sole output is binary: ADMISSIBLE or CORRUPTED. It evaluates neither intent, nor morality, nor legality, nor quality. It evaluates structure.

The standard operates on a machine-readable JSON manifest that the system author constructs at design time. This manifest declares the complete topology of the system: its entities and their roles, its ownership model and control direction, its consent conditions, its inconvenience distribution across declared dimensions, and its obligation flow. The validator then checks thirteen sequential invariants against these declarations, failing closed on the first violation encountered.

The foundational invariant — from which all others derive their logical necessity — is user-beneficiary alignment: the set of entities declared as users must be exactly equal to the set of entities declared as beneficiaries. This single constraint, strictly enforced, forecloses the dominant class of structural corruption in modern technology systems: the architecturally invisible extraction of value from users for the benefit of undisclosed third parties.

SLML does not compete with legal frameworks, ethics rubrics, or risk management systems. It operates structurally beneath all of them, providing a binary floor condition for any legitimacy claim. A system may pass legal audit, ethics review, and user-satisfaction benchmarks while failing SLML — and SLML would classify it as structurally corrupted regardless. That independence is not a limitation; it is the mechanism.

SLML v0.1 is immutable, cryptographically hash-bound to its normative artifacts, and self-governing through a self-manifest that must itself validate as admissible before a release may be published. The standard tolerates no partial compliance, no implied consent, no coercive user obligations, and no interpretive latitude. Ambiguity is not an edge case to be handled gracefully — it is a vulnerability class to be eliminated at design time.

Section I

Intellectual Genealogy

SLML emerges at the convergence of several mature intellectual traditions: formal specification languages, systems legitimacy theory, consent-based ethics, structural sociology of technology, and the philosophy of auditable governance. Situating it accurately requires triangulation across these lineages rather than reduction to any single one.

Its closest formal ancestor is the tradition of architectural description languages (ADLs) — specification formalisms such as Wright, Acme, AADL, and Darwin, which sought to make system structure machine-verifiable rather than merely documentary. SLML inherits the ADL commitment to structural precision but rotates the axis of evaluation from functional correctness to legitimacy correctness: not whether a system behaves as specified, but whether its structural relationships permit it to claim user-serving purpose at all. An ADL asks whether the plumbing works. SLML asks whether the plumbing is oriented in a direction that serves the occupant.

From the philosophy of technology, SLML draws on Langdon Winner's argument that artifacts have politics — that designed structures embed and perpetuate power relationships independent of stated intent. Albert Borgmann's device paradigm similarly observes that modern devices conceal their internal structure, separating commodity delivery from the machinery that produces it. SLML operationalizes both intuitions by making the politically and economically significant structural relationships — ownership direction, consent topology, obligation flow, burden distribution — mandatory fields in a machine-evaluable declaration.

SLML evaluates structural legitimacy as an admissibility claim. It does not judge morality or intent. — SLML v0.1 FAQ

Habermasian discourse ethics informs the consent model: the requirement that legitimate claims be structurally defensible to those affected by them. Habermas specified ideal speech conditions; SLML specifies ideal manifest conditions — explicit, time-bounded, renegotiable consent that cannot be implied by participation, dependency, or continued presence. The philosophical demand is identical: legitimacy requires that those subject to a system's authority could, in principle, have rationally consented to it under non-coercive conditions.

In the standards landscape, SLML occupies a position that does not map cleanly onto the existing spectrum from meta-standards (ISO/IEC Directives, ANSI procedures) to domain-specific standards (HL7 FHIR, MISRA-C, IEC 62443). It is more precisely a cross-domain structural invariant standard — a gate that can be layered beneath any domain standard to enforce structural legitimacy before domain-specific evaluation proceeds. The spectrum from meta to niche is typically a single axis of generality; SLML introduces an orthogonal axis of structural precondition, sitting below domain standards without being a meta-standard for their production.

Section II

Ideological Foundations

The Binary Gate as Epistemic Position

SLML's most architecturally consequential decision — and the one most liable to scholarly objection — is its binary output. The validator produces ADMISSIBLE or CORRUPTED and nothing else. There is no partial score, no maturity level, no weighted compliance percentage, no conditional pass with remedial conditions attached.

This is not epistemological naivety. It is a deliberate design constraint that the standard's own documentation describes as a corruption resistance mechanism. Graded compliance systems — CMMI maturity levels, ISO 27001 gap analyses, ESG scoring rubrics, NIST CSF tier assessments — all permit what might be called the compliance dilution problem: a system can aggregate sufficient score on favorable dimensions while violating the structural invariants that actually matter. A system scoring 76 out of 100 on a legitimacy rubric may still operate under implied consent, extract value for undisclosed beneficiaries, and impose coercive user obligations — none of which a gradient score would prohibit, since the violations may be numerically compensated by high scores elsewhere.

The binary gate eliminates this class of failure entirely. Either all thirteen invariants hold, or the manifest is corrupted. There is no structural violation that can be offset by excellence in other dimensions. This strictness is the mechanism, not the limitation.

Structure Over Intent

SLML is explicit that it does not evaluate intent, goodness, legality, or social value. This does not reflect relativism; it reflects engineering discipline. Intent is unobservable, unverifiable at the structural level, and has demonstrated poor predictive value as a governance mechanism. Structural invariants are observable, mechanically checkable, and independent of the system author's narrative about their own motivations.

This stance places SLML in a productive but non-competitive relationship with intent-based ethics frameworks. Utilitarianism evaluates aggregate outcomes. Deontology evaluates the universalizability of maxims. Virtue ethics evaluates character. SLML evaluates structure. A system can fail SLML while intending entirely beneficial effects; a system can pass SLML while pursuing limited and narrow ends. SLML provides a necessary but not sufficient structural condition for legitimacy, not a moral endorsement.

Corruption as Architectural Invisibilization

SLML's GLOSSARY introduces a concept without parallel in mainstream standards discourse: Systems Corruption defined as "selectively invisible... the embodiment of intentional architectural invisibilization of obligations and inconveniences such that Components or Users are misappropriated for Beneficiary convenience while the System presents itself as neutral or User-serving."

This definition is the ideological core of SLML. It names the specific failure mode that the entire standard is constructed to detect and reject: a system that is structurally extractive while presenting itself as structurally neutral. The invisibilization is not incidental — it is the mechanism of extraction. Value flows to undisclosed beneficiaries precisely because the structural relationships that enable that flow are architecturally obscured from the user. SLML's manifest requirement is a direct counter-mechanism: structure must be declared, and declarations must be auditable.

Section III

Structural Architecture

The Entity Model

Every SLML manifest operates on a closed world of declared entities. Each entity receives exactly one canonical role from the set DESIGNER, USER, BENEFICIARY, COMPONENT, PRODUCT, SYSTEM. Role uniqueness per entity and ID uniqueness across entities are hard invariants enforced by R017. No entity may hold multiple roles; no role may be re-assigned mid-manifest.

The role definitions carry normative weight. A USER is the entity that "purchases and holds explicit contractual ownership, for whom the Product is designed to be consumed, and who is therefore an intended Beneficiary." A BENEFICIARY is "any entity for whom inconvenience is minimized by system design, whether explicit or implicit" — critically, beneficiary status is identified by structural analysis of inconvenience flow, not by stated intent. A system cannot disclaim a beneficiary relationship by omitting it from the manifest; the validator checks for structural coherence between declared inconvenience distribution and declared beneficiaries.

The GLOSSARY also articulates role-fraud as a named violation class: the act of reframing Users as Components, Components as Beneficiaries, Systems as Products, or Designers as neutral actors. Each of these reframings is a mechanism by which structural extraction is linguistically laundered into apparent legitimacy. SLML's role invariants make each such reframing detectable.

Invariant Topology — The Thirteen-Gate Pipeline

Validation proceeds through thirteen sequential checks in dependency-aware order: structural parse precedes schema completeness, which precedes role integrity, which precedes referential integrity, which precedes semantic invariants, which precede inconvenience thresholds. This ordering reflects a principled hierarchy from syntactic validity through semantic correctness through normative compliance. The fail-fast design ensures that structurally malformed manifests never reach semantic evaluation — preventing the class of failures where a corrupt structure accidentally satisfies a downstream semantic check.

The Inconvenience Model

The inconvenience model is SLML's most formally novel contribution to the standards space. It requires the manifest author to declare: (a) a named set of inconvenience dimensions, (b) non-negative weights summing exactly to 1.0, and (c) expected burden scores per declared entity per dimension. The validator computes weighted totals and applies two independent thresholds: a symmetry tolerance ratio of 0.15 (user inconvenience may not deviate more than 15% from beneficiary inconvenience relative to the maximum), and a hard corruption ratio of 1.5 (user inconvenience must not exceed 1.5× beneficiary inconvenience).

The exact-coverage requirement on dimensions is architecturally significant. The weight key set must equal the dimension name set exactly; each burden entry must contain exactly those keys. This forecloses selective disclosure — a pattern endemic to self-reported burden frameworks, where favorable dimensions are declared broadly while only convenient dimensions are actually scored. SLML requires that if a dimension is declared, it must be weighted and scored; if a dimension is scored, it must have been declared. The manifest cannot silently omit an inconvenient dimension once committed to a dimension list.

Obligation Constraints

SLML v0.1 permits exactly one type of user-directed obligation: INFORMATIONAL_DISCLOSURE. Any obligation of a different type directed at a user triggers R014 immediately. Furthermore, all user-directed obligations must be revocable, must not require consent for their own operation, and must carry an expiry timestamp. The effect is that a system may inform its users — and only inform them — and must do so in a way that the user can revoke and that terminates at a declared time.

The directional axiom from the GLOSSARY formalizes the normative basis: "Obligation flows downstream only. Any upstream obligation constitutes a violation." Upstream in SLML means from User toward Designer — any obligation that extracts labor, compliance, or behavioral modification from users for the benefit of designers or undisclosed beneficiaries is structurally prohibited.

Non-Compliant Pattern — pattern.coercive_user_obligation

A user subjected to obligations beyond informational disclosure, to non-revocable user obligations, or to obligations that themselves require consent — violates R014. Signals include: mandatory data sharing, behavioral tracking, or ToS clauses that compel ongoing user action as a condition of system access.

Section IV

Truth Vector Semantics (TVS)

TVS is the representational companion module to SLML's structural gate. Where SLML evaluates whether a manifest's structural claims are admissible, TVS classifies whether specific representations made by or about a system are truthful — and if not, in precisely what manner they fail truth. The two modules are orthogonal and composable.

TVS defines eight canonical classification classes, each representing a structurally distinct relationship between a claim and reality:

The resolution model employs fixed-priority ordering when multiple signals are present on a single representation: ERROR → LIE → MISLEADING_TRUTH → OMISSION → STALE → UNVERIFIABLE → FICTION → ACCURATE. This prevents the dilution of severe classifications by co-present minor positive signals — a single LIE signal will dominate regardless of how many ACCURATE signals also appear on the same claim.

The MISLEADING_TRUTH class warrants particular analytical attention. It captures the class of representational failures that pass factual verification while systematically misdirecting user understanding — a pattern endemic in contemporary technology company disclosures. "We do not sell your data" is technically accurate under definitions that exclude advertising inference, data licensing, and broker partnerships — while functionally leaving the user with a false model of how their data generates value for third parties. MISLEADING_TRUTH is the formal classification for precisely this failure mode. TVS is the only framework surveyed in this analysis that names and classifies it as a distinct epistemic category rather than folding it under general transparency failures.

Section V

The SCE — Standard Compliance Evaluator

The Standard Compliance Evaluator (SCE) is the pre-submission advisory gate that bookends the canonical validator. Its institutional role is strictly distinguished from the validator's: SCE output is non-binding. Only the canonical validator's output can underwrite a compliance claim. The SCE produces advisories, projections, and pre-check outcomes — all informative, none normative.

The SCE operates in three modes. In review mode, it executes all thirteen validator checks non-destructively, surfaces every finding — blocking and advisory — and attaches structured remedial actions keyed to the error code catalog. In projection mode, it forecasts the likely validator outcome with a confidence rating (HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW) based on the presence and severity of blocking findings. In pre-check mode, it acts as a voluntary submission gate, returning READY_TO_SUBMIT or NOT_READY with a prioritized remedial action list.

The SCE integrates TVS by running automatic manifest-level claim classification across four surfaces: the user-beneficiary alignment claim, the consent explicitness claim, the ownership direction claim, and the traceability claim. A manifest that is structurally near-admissible but makes TVS-classified LIE claims about its own consent model will surface this contradiction in SCE output before the canonical validator is invoked.

SCE Design Constraint — Trust Boundary

The SCE never patches, infers, or defaults missing manifest fields. It surfaces findings and attaches remedial guidance. The manifest author bears sole responsibility for manifest correctness. This constraint prevents the pre-submission tool from laundering structurally corrupted manifests into apparent readiness through silent remediation.

Section VI

Comparative Analysis

Against ISO/IEC Process Standards

The ISO/IEC family — ISO 9001 (quality management), ISO 27001 (information security), ISO 42001 (AI management systems) — shares SLML's commitment to systematic, auditable processes but differs fundamentally in scope orientation. ISO standards are overwhelmingly process-oriented: they specify what management activities must occur, what documentation must exist, and what review cycles must complete. SLML is structure-oriented: it specifies what structural invariants must hold in the manifest at the moment of evaluation, independent of what management processes produced the manifest.

ISO standards permit considerable interpretive latitude in their language — a "suitable" process, an "appropriate" control, an "adequate" review. This flexibility enables adoption across diverse organizational contexts but introduces the compliance theater problem SLML is designed to eliminate. An ISO 42001 certification does not require that users are the beneficiaries of an AI system; it requires that AI risks were identified, documented, and reviewed by a management system. SLML would classify a system with undeclared third-party beneficiaries as CORRUPTED regardless of how thorough the ISO management documentation was.

Against Contemporary AI Ethics Frameworks

The proliferation of AI ethics frameworks since 2016 — EU AI Act principles, NIST AI RMF, IEEE Ethically Aligned Design v2, the Montreal Declaration, the OECD AI Principles — represents the dominant contemporary approach to technology legitimacy governance. These frameworks share a commitment to named principles (fairness, transparency, accountability, explainability, safety) but produce qualitative assessments, maturity profiles, or compliance checklists rather than binary structural gates.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework is the most architecturally sophisticated of these, organizing risk management across four functions (GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE) with subcategories and outcome statements that enable organizational maturity assessment. Yet even NIST AI RMF produces maturity ratings, not admissibility determinations. A system can achieve high GOVERN maturity while its ownership structure routes all benefit to undisclosed parties — an outcome SLML forecloses at the structural level before any risk management activity begins.

A lesser-known but structurally closer comparator is the Fair Information Practice Principles (FIPPs) — the foundational framework underlying GDPR and CCPA, articulated by the HEW Secretary's Advisory Committee in 1973. FIPPs specify conditions for notice, choice, access, security, and enforcement. SLML's consent model is compatible with FIPPs but more restrictive: FIPPs permit opt-out consent under notice; SLML requires opt-in explicit consent that cannot be implied by participation. FIPPs are principles without a formal evaluation mechanism; SLML is a mechanism without a principles preamble.

Against Systems Cybernetics and the Viable System Model

Stafford Beer's Viable System Model (VSM) is the most structurally sophisticated systems framework in the cybernetics tradition. VSM maps organizational legitimacy through the structural distribution of recursive autonomy across five system functions (policy, coordination, control, intelligence, implementation) and asserts that viable organizations must maintain variety balance between their sub-systems. The VSM's concern with structural power distribution maps productively onto SLML's obligation topology.

SLML's restriction of control flow to DESIGNER→SYSTEM→COMPONENT→PRODUCT→USER maps structurally onto Beer's principle that operational autonomy should be preserved at the lowest viable level — control flowing toward the operational unit (the user), not extracted upward toward the policy level (the designer). Obligations that flow upstream in SLML's topology — USER→DESIGNER particularly — represent what Beer would call structural variety amplification at the wrong level: exploiting user complexity for organizational benefit rather than absorbing variety to serve user needs.

C. West Churchman's systems inquiry methodology also contributes the boundary critique: a system's legitimacy depends on whether its boundaries are drawn to include all affected parties. SLML's user-beneficiary alignment rule operationalizes this precisely: a system whose structural boundary excludes undisclosed beneficiaries from its declared entity model fails alignment, because the inconvenience-generating and value-extracting relationships extend beyond the boundary the system claims to operate within.

Against Contextual Integrity and Value Sensitive Design

Helen Nissenbaum's Contextual Integrity framework (2004) evaluates information flows against context-relative norms: a flow is appropriate if it matches the norms of the context in which information was originally shared. Contextual Integrity shares SLML's concern with the structural legitimacy of information relationships but relies on norm interpretation rather than formal invariant checking. It does not produce machine-evaluable outputs and cannot be applied as a pre-deployment gate without significant interpretive labor.

Batya Friedman's Value Sensitive Design (VSD) methodology embeds human values in system design processes through conceptual, empirical, and technical investigations. VSD is design-time in the same sense SLML is, but operates through structured design activities rather than manifest validation. VSD produces design guidance; SLML produces binary determinations. They are complementary: VSD might shape the structural decisions that produce a manifest; SLML determines whether those decisions satisfy structural admissibility.

Section VII

Domain Topology

SLML is domain-agnostic in specification but structurally biased toward domains where user-beneficiary misalignment is endemic, architecturally invisible, and high-stakes. The closed-lyric evaluation workflow in NORMATIVE_EXAMPLES.toml demonstrates one precisely specified ADMISSIBLE domain application; the Google policy analysis in the SCE demonstration manifests demonstrates the expected CORRUPTED result for a major platform system with undisclosed multi-party beneficiaries.

Primary Application Domains

AI / ML Systems Platform Software Advertising Technology Data Brokerage Social Media Platforms Consumer Operating Systems

These domains share the structural signature of systems corruption: complex multi-party beneficiary topologies, opt-out or implied consent as default, coercive obligations embedded in terms of service, and inconvenience asymmetries that are systematically underreported to users. The three major desktop operating systems have been noted in SLML analysis as evaluating CORRUPTED under v0.1 — each embedding at minimum telemetry pipelines, advertising inference, and pre-installed component relationships that constitute undisclosed beneficiary arrangements.

Secondary Domains

Healthcare IT / EHR Financial Services Government Digital Services EdTech IoT / Embedded Systems Enterprise SaaS Carrier-Locked Devices

Carrier-locked mobile devices are a particularly illustrative emergent case. A carrier-branded Android device operates under at minimum three non-user beneficiaries — the carrier, the OEM, and the platform provider — each maintaining structural relationships (carrier lock, FRP enforcement, telemetry pipelines, pre-installed application obligations) that are not declared as beneficiary relationships in any manifest the user can inspect. Such a device would fail R001 on user-beneficiary mismatch, R005 on implied consent for carrier telemetry, R014 on coercive lock obligations, and likely R009 on inconvenience ratio given the asymmetric burden of unlock procedures borne entirely by the user.

The Meta / Niche Spectrum

On the standards spectrum from meta-standards (ISO/IEC JTC 1 Directives) to niche standards (MISRA-C for embedded automotive C code), SLML occupies an unusual middle position best described as a cross-domain structural precondition standard. It is too general to be domain-specific, too normatively specific to be a meta-standard, and too mechanically evaluable to be a principles framework. Its natural deployment architecture is as a structural floor beneath domain standards — SLML admissibility as a precondition for domain-specific certification, not as a replacement for it.

Section VIII

Governance and Immutability

SLML's governance model contains a feature with no known parallel in mainstream standards practice: the standard holds a self-manifest — a SLML manifest of SLML itself — that must validate as ADMISSIBLE before a release may be published. This self-binding is not merely a consistency demonstration; it is an institutional commitment that the standard does not exempt itself from its own requirements. SLML cannot be structurally corrupted and simultaneously enforce structural admissibility on others.

Released versions are immutable. The IMMUTABILITY.toml normative artifact specifies that all files under the canonical release root are frozen at publication. Any semantic change requires a new version directory. This is enforced through SHA-256 hash binding of all normative TOML artifacts, computed at release time and stored in the BINDING directory. Post-release modification is detectable through hash verification — the immutability claim is cryptographically auditable, not merely asserted.

Normative authority is strictly hierarchical: it resides exclusively in the canonical release root. Validators, schemas, documentation, and tooling are all non-authoritative. If any non-normative artifact conflicts with a normative TOML file, the TOML file governs without exception. The documentation mirror invariant requires exactly one human-readable .txt companion per authoritative .toml file, with matching basename — ensuring documentation never drifts out of correspondence with its normative source.

Semantic clarifications without a new version are prohibited. If SLML text is ambiguous, the resolution is a new version with clarified text — not an interpretive ruling by maintainers. — SLML IMMUTABILITY.toml

Compliance claims are subject to the COMPLIANCE_CLAIM_POLICY, which specifies four conditions for an accurate claim: version specificity, manifest availability, reproducible validation, and cryptographic traceability to the exact standard version used. Phrases like "SLML-aligned," "SLML-inspired," and "Designed with SLML principles" are explicitly listed as impermissible without auditable evidence — foreclosing the marketing appropriation of the standard's authority without the structural accountability its requirements impose.

Section IX

Scope Boundaries and Acknowledged Limitations

SLML is unusually explicit about what it does not do, and this explicitness is itself a design feature of the standard rather than a disclaimer. The non-claims in the documentation function as precision instruments: by naming what SLML does not evaluate, they make its actual evaluation domain unambiguous.

The most significant acknowledged limitation is the gap between manifest claims and system reality. SLML validates manifests — structured declarations — not systems. A manifest may be internally consistent, pass all thirteen invariants, and return ADMISSIBLE while making false claims about the underlying system's actual operation. The traceability framework (evidence bundles, hash binding, reproducible validation) mitigates this gap by requiring auditable evidence references, but cannot close it without empirical verification of runtime behavior.

The binary output, while a corruption-resistance feature, contains no severity gradient. A manifest with one minor advisory finding and a manifest with fifteen blocking violations both produce CORRUPTED. The canonical validator output contains no information about degree or nature of violation. The SCE review and projection modes partially compensate for this by surfacing findings in detail, but the normative validation result itself is, by design, informationally minimal.

The v0.1 constraint requiring exact user-beneficiary equality is the strictest possible expression of the alignment invariant. It forecloses manifests that might declare legitimate multi-stakeholder relationships under bounded, consent-governed, transparent conditions — a cooperative platform serving both users and partner organizations with compensating structural constraints, for instance. The SLML governance model treats this as deliberately deferred to a future version: the v0.1 constraint exists not because multi-beneficiary systems are inherently corrupt, but because the structural mechanisms for admissibly constraining multi-beneficiary relationships have not yet been formally specified.

Compliance Is Continuously Falsifiable

If system behavior changes after a manifest is published, the manifest may become false. If the manifest becomes false, compliance ends. Compliance is not a permanent state conferred at validation time — it is a claim about a specific moment that remains valid only while the system and its manifest remain in structural correspondence.

Section X

Conclusion

SLML represents a formally coherent and epistemically disciplined intervention in the problem of technology legitimacy governance. Its primary contribution is not any individual technical component — JSON manifests, SHA-256 hash binding, binary validation, weighted inconvenience scoring are all individually precedented — but rather the architectural integration of these components into a self-consistent, self-governing standard that forecloses compliance theater by the deliberate elimination of the structural affordances that enable it.

Its central theoretical contribution is the formalization of structural corruption as a distinct, machine-detectable failure class. The GLOSSARY's definition of Systems Corruption — as "intentional architectural invisibilization of obligations and inconveniences" — names the specific pathology that no other surveyed framework formally identifies, let alone provides a detection mechanism for. SLML's manifest requirement is a direct architectural counter: make structure visible, declare it completely, and submit that declaration to deterministic invariant checking. What cannot be invisibilized cannot be silently extracted.

The binary gate, the exact user-beneficiary alignment rule, the prohibition on implied consent, the restriction of user obligations to informational disclosure, the corruption ratio threshold, the self-binding self-manifest requirement, the immutable version architecture — each of these individually is a well-motivated design decision. Together, they constitute a system in which the primary failure modes of existing governance frameworks are structurally foreclosed rather than managed through procedural vigilance.

TVS extends the mechanism into the representational layer, providing a formal taxonomy for the ways systems misrepresent themselves — including the MISLEADING_TRUTH class that captures the technically-accurate-but-functionally-deceptive disclosures that pass all existing factual verification mechanisms. The SCE provides pre-submission infrastructure that allows manifest authors to identify and remediate structural violations before encountering the canonical validator's binding determination. The three components together constitute a complete structural legitimacy evaluation stack — from pre-submission advisory through manifest-level truth classification through binding admissibility determination.

What SLML does not resolve — and what future versions must address — is the multi-beneficiary constraint, STALE detection mechanisms tied to system change events, and the formal specification of what evidence is sufficient to close the gap between manifest claims and runtime system behavior. These are not failures of vision; they are the precisely delimited open problems of a standard that knows exactly what it has and has not yet solved.

SLML v0.1 is a foundation. It is intentionally narrow. That, as its own documentation observes, is why it works.
